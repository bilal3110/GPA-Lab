Understanding GPA vs. CGPA: Key Differences

Many students confuse GPA (Grade Point Average) with CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average). While both measure academic achievement on a 4.0 scale, they operate on different scopes:

Feature Semester GPA (SGPA) Cumulative GPA (CGPA)
Definition Grade point average earned during a single academic term or semester. Overall grade point average accumulated across all completed semesters.
Calculation Scope Courses taken in one specific semester only. Every credit-bearing course or semester completed in the degree program.
Weighting Basis Weighted by individual course credit hours. Weighted by semester credit hours (or all cumulative course credits).
Academic Impact Determines semester honors, Dean's List eligibility, or semester probation. Determines degree classification, graduation honors, scholarships, and graduate school admission.
Volatility High: Changes significantly with each individual course grade. Stabilizes over time: Later semesters have less percentage impact as total credits grow.

To learn more about semester-specific formulas, read our dedicated Semester GPA guide, or visit the CGPA Calculator guide to understand multi-semester credit weighting.

Standard 4.0 Grading Scale Guide: Pakistan (HEC) & International Standards

In Pakistan, the Higher Education Commission (HEC) establishes semester system guidelines followed by top institutions such as NUST, FAST-NUCES, LUMS, COMSATS, UET, Punjab University, IBA Karachi, and GIKI. Internationally, universities in the United States, Canada, the Middle East, and beyond employ comparable 4.0 quality-point systems.

GPA Lab uses the standard 4.0 scale by default:

University Attendance Rules & The 75% Requirement

At most universities across Pakistan and internationally, maintaining a minimum attendance rate—typically 75% or 80%—is mandatory to sit for midterm and final examinations. Falling below this threshold can result in an "F Attendance" (FA) grade or debarment from exams.

GPA Lab’s Attendance Tracker solves two vital questions:

  1. "What is my current percentage?" Derived by dividing attended classes by total classes held to date.
  2. "How many consecutive classes must I attend to reach my goal?" For example, if you are currently at 68% and need 75% before the semester ends, our calculator computes the exact number of upcoming lectures you must attend without missing a single one.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How is CGPA calculated from individual semesters?

Cumulative GPA is calculated as a credit-weighted average across all completed semesters. Multiply each semester's GPA by that semester's total credit hours to find its quality points. Sum all quality points together and divide by the total cumulative credit hours completed. Taking a simple arithmetic average of GPAs yields an incorrect result whenever semesters have different credit hour totals.

What is the difference between GPA and CGPA?

Semester GPA (SGPA) represents your academic performance in a single term, calculated from that term's course grades and credits. Cumulative GPA (CGPA) reflects your entire academic journey across all completed semesters in your degree program.

How does the attendance target calculator determine required classes?

The calculator uses the consecutive attendance formula: Required Classes = ceil((Target% × Total Held - 100 × Attended) / (100 - Target%)). It calculates the exact number of future lectures you must attend consecutively to raise your cumulative attendance percentage to your desired benchmark.

Does GPA Lab store or transmit my grades?

No. GPA Lab runs entirely in your web browser. All entered data is stored securely in your browser's private localStorage. No personal information or academic records are ever transmitted to any external server or third party.

Does GPA Lab support university grading systems in Pakistan and abroad?

Yes. GPA Lab natively supports the standard 4.0 grading scale recommended by the Higher Education Commission (HEC) of Pakistan, which aligns with international 4.0 semester systems used in North America, Europe, and Asia.
